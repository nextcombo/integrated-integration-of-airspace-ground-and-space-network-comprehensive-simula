# Integrated Integration of Airspace, Ground, and Space Network Comprehensive Simulation Platform [with C+Python Code]

SAGIN (Sky-Air-Ground Integrated Network) is envisaged as a promising solution that offers economical, efficient, large-scale, and flexible wireless coverage and communication services. Due to the practical deployment of SAGIN tests being both challenging and daunting, an efficient SAGIN simulation platform is required. In this paper, we introduce our developed SAGIN simulation platform that supports various mobility trajectories and protocols for space, aviation, and ground networks. It enables centralized and decentralized controllers to optimize network functions such as access control and resource orchestration. Moreover, various interfaces extend the platform's functionality to facilitate user-defined mobile tracking and control algorithms. We also present a case study where highly mobile vehicle users dynamically select different wireless access networks based on their Quality of Service (QoS) requirements.
The future of communication networks aims to support a variety of promising services and applications by providing ubiquitous connectivity, high data rates, low latency, and high reliability. Current ground-based communication networks, such as WiFi, Long-Term Evolution (LTE), and the emerging fifth-generation (5G) network, provide large-capacity data pipelines through the adoption of cutting-edge communication and network technologies; they not only can further enhance to meet the growing service demands but also improve cost-effectiveness. On the one hand, deploying LTE or 5G infrastructure to provide network coverage for rural mobile users and remote Internet of Things (IoT) devices could lead to severe inefficiencies in cost-effectiveness. For example, in the UK, by 2030, the total costs of 5G, including capital expenditure (CapEx) and operational expenditure (OpEx), will only be 21% of the cost for urban and suburban populations of 70.5%, while the total costs for 5G services for 29.5% of rural population will be 79% [1]. On the other hand, even in urban areas, significant changes in time and space may occur due to events like sports competitions and user mobility (such as daily vehicle traffic variations). The fixed deployment of ground-based communication networks is not flexible for on-demand services [2] and [3].
The Integrated Space-Aerial Network (SAGIN) is envisioned as a key solution for providing extensive coverage and further enhancing network performance [4] and [5]. SAGIN consists of three layers: the spatial layer, which includes communication satellites; the aerial layer, which includes airborne communication devices such as balloons and unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs); and the ground layer, which includes ground communication networks and users. With SAGIN, the high costs of 5G networks can be significantly reduced, as satellites can effectively cover vast rural areas. Typically, low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellites can cover up to 7.95% of the Earth's surface area, while geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO) satellites can cover nearly half of the Earth's surface area, providing access capabilities for rural users or widespread deployment of Internet of Things (IoT) devices. The spatial layer could become a promising candidate for emergency public services or network control. By flexibly scheduling drones to provide time-coverage enhancement, the dynamic data traffic demands in space and time can be met. Additionally, by leveraging the flexibility and controllable mobility of drones, many network functions and applications can be enabled, such as edge computing, caching, and IoT services.
In the drive of immense potential, both academia and industry are increasingly focusing on the research and development of SAGIN. The industry has been deploying space and airborne communication networks, especially in recent years. Google's Loon and AT&T's Flying COW use balloons and drones to provide high-speed wireless internet access, while SpaceX is developing Starlink to build low-cost and high-performance LEO satellite access networks. Research related to SAGIN has also been thriving recently [6]. As an indispensable tool in the research and development of communication networks, simulation platforms play a crucial role in analyzing and evaluating networks, especially in SAGIN, where actual deployment and testing can be very expensive. Although some simulation software can be used independently with satellite networks or ground networks, there has not yet been developed a comprehensive SAGIN simulation platform that addresses the challenges of integrating space, air, and ground networks, which motivates our work.
In this paper, we propose a comprehensive SAGIN simulation platform and provide a case study on heterogeneous vehicle access control using the platform. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a SAGIN simulation platform that integrates multiple network protocols, node mobility, and control algorithms. However, research on SAGIN is still in its early stages, so the SAGIN simulation platform needs further improvement to simulate emerging SAGIN architectures, protocols, and applications. In this section, we will discuss several themes for future research and development of the SAGIN simulation platform.
Software-Defined Networking (SDN) and Network Functions Virtualization (NFV) technologies have significantly transformed the architecture of communication networks, transitioning from dedicated hardware to commercial hardware and from rigid to flexible and programmable functions. The flexibility and scalability brought by SDN/NFV are particularly beneficial for SAGIN, which features heterogeneous network architectures, multidimensional resources, and stringent business requirements [14]. However, due to the high complexity of SAGIN, using a single central controller may be inefficient, potentially leading to control signal overload and delayed response to network events. Therefore, deploying an SDN controller within SAGIN and coordinating between controllers requires careful consideration. To evaluate the performance of SAGIN supporting SDN/NFV, some open-source frameworks and protocols can be implemented in simulation platforms, such as OpenFlow, OpenStack, Open-daylight, Ryuk control framework, etc. However, there are some challenging issues. For example, network slicing requires resource isolation, and implementing multidimensional resource isolation in the SAGIN simulation platform involves modifying the NS-3 basic functional modules, which requires further research.
Cloud/edge computing can be integrated into SAGIN to meet the diverse needs of countless services and applications. With enhanced coverage and network flexibility provided by satellites and drones, SAGIN can provide cloud services, compute offloading, and edge caching in remote areas and demanding regions. In simulation platforms, implementing cloud/edge technology brings technical challenges, such as modeling and simulating cloud data centers and distributed edge servers, virtualizing server hosts and resources, supporting dynamic simulation elements (satellites, drones, and mobile users), and user-defined strategies.
